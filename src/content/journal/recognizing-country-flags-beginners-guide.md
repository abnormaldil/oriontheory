There are close to two hundred national flags, and trying to memorize them one by one is the slowest way to learn them. A better approach is to notice that most flags are built from a small number of shared layouts. Once you can name the layout, you’ve already narrowed a guess down to a handful of countries.

## Two words worth knowing

Flag designers describe a flag in terms of the **hoist** — the half nearest the flagpole — and the **fly**, the half that flutters free. “Green at the hoist” simply means the green stripe sits next to the pole. Keeping hoist and fly in mind helps with flags that use the same colours in a different order.

## Start with the layout

Before you look at colours, ask what shape the flag is built from.

- **Horizontal bands.** Germany (black, red, gold), the Netherlands (red, white, blue) and Russia (white, blue, red) all use three horizontal stripes.
- **Vertical bands.** France, Italy, Ireland and Belgium use three vertical stripes, often called a tricolour.
- **Nordic cross.** Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland and Iceland share a cross that sits closer to the hoist than the fly.
- **Canton.** A canton is a rectangle in the upper hoist corner. The United States, Malaysia and Greece all have one, as do Australia and New Zealand.
- **Disc.** Japan and Bangladesh place a single disc on a plain field.
- **Hoist triangle.** The Czech Republic, the Philippines, Cuba and the Bahamas all have a triangle pointing in from the hoist.

## Then read the colours, then the order

Layout narrows the field; colour and order usually finish the job. Ireland and Côte d’Ivoire both use green, white and orange vertical stripes, but Ireland has green at the hoist while Côte d’Ivoire has orange there. Indonesia and Poland are both red and white bicolours, but Indonesia is red over white and Poland is white over red.

## Finally, check the details

Some pairs are close enough that you need a second look.

- **Chad and Romania** both have blue, yellow and red vertical stripes. They are famously hard to tell apart; Chad’s blue is usually shown slightly darker.
- **Indonesia and Monaco** are both red over white. The difference is the shape: Indonesia’s flag is longer (2:3), Monaco’s is closer to square (4:5).
- **Italy and Mexico** share green, white and red vertical stripes, but Mexico’s flag carries its coat of arms in the centre.
- **Australia and New Zealand** both have a blue field with a canton and the stars of the Southern Cross. Australia’s stars are white; New Zealand’s are red with white borders.

## A simple routine

When you meet an unfamiliar flag, try asking four questions in order: *What is the layout? What are the colours? What order are they in? Is there an emblem or detail that breaks the tie?* Most of the time you will be down to one or two countries before you reach the last question.

That routine is part of what makes flags fun to learn — and it’s the kind of thinking we want What Flag to reward.
