This Privacy Policy explains how **What Flag** (Android package `com.whatflag.app`), published by Orion Theory, handles information.

What Flag is an offline-first geography game. The app's own code does not operate user accounts or a backend. Gameplay progress is stored on the device. The game uses Google Mobile Ads for rewarded and interstitial ads and Google Play Billing for its planned one-time premium purchase. Those third-party services have their own privacy practices.

## 2. Information handled by What Flag

### Information stored on your device

The game stores gameplay and settings locally. Depending on your progress and choices, this can include:

- level progress, scores, stars, coins, achievements, and collection progress;
- game settings and selected explorer character;
- reminder preferences and scheduled reminder information;
- locally maintained advertising counters and a premium-status flag;
- device time-zone information used to schedule local reminders.

This information is stored in app-private Hive storage. The game does not require you to create an account, provide your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos, or precise location.

The app does not encrypt its Hive data itself. Android's device-level protections may apply. Do not treat locally stored game data as a secure vault for sensitive information.

### Information sent to Orion Theory

The app does not send gameplay data to an Orion Theory server. What Flag has no app backend, account system, analytics service, or crash-reporting service operated by Orion Theory.

## 3. Google advertising

What Flag uses the Google Mobile Ads SDK to show:

- **Rewarded ads:** a player may choose to watch an available ad to earn 50 in-game coins. The reward is granted only after the ad reports that the reward has been earned. The game limits rewarded ads locally to three per day.
- **Interstitial ads:** full-screen ads may appear at certain level-result points, subject to the game's frequency rules.

The current version does not implement banner ads. Ad availability and format may change.

Google's advertising services may process information such as IP address (which may be used to infer approximate location), ad interactions, diagnostics, and device or app identifiers, as described in Google's own documentation and policies. This information may be processed by Google and its service providers for advertising delivery, measurement, fraud prevention, and related purposes.

The current configuration applies child-directed and under-age-of-consent treatment to ad requests and sets a G content rating. The advertising ID permission is removed from the release manifest. These settings do not mean that the Google advertising SDK processes no information.

Google privacy resources:

- [Google Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy)
- [How Google uses information from apps and sites that use its services](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites)

## 4. Google Play purchases

The game contains code for a planned one-time, non-consumable premium purchase with product ID `whatflag_premium_lifetime`. The project shows a fallback price of ₹299, but the product had not been created in Google Play when this notice was prepared. Therefore, this notice does not represent that the purchase is currently available or that ₹299 is its final price.

If and when the purchase is offered, Google Play processes the transaction under its own terms. What Flag stores a local premium-status flag and does not send payment details to an Orion Theory server. The planned premium benefits include removing ads and doubling certain coin rewards. Product availability, localized price, and benefits will be shown in the app or Google Play at the time of purchase.

See [Google Play's terms](https://play.google.com/about/play-terms/) and [Google's Privacy Policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

## 5. Local notifications

What Flag can schedule optional reminders on your device, such as daily play reminders and reminders after a period of inactivity.

- Reminders are off by default.
- The game asks for notification permission when you choose to enable reminders, where Android requires it.
- Notifications are scheduled locally; the game does not use Firebase Cloud Messaging or a remote push-token system.
- You can disable reminders in the game's settings or your Android notification settings.

Reminder times and related preferences are stored locally.

## 6. Android backup and retention

Game data is stored locally and is generally retained until you clear the app's data or uninstall the game. However, the app's Android configuration does not disable Android Auto Backup. As a result, Android may back up eligible app data to the user's Google account and restore it under Android's backup rules.

This operating-system backup is not a cloud-save service operated by Orion Theory. Its availability and handling depend on Android and Google settings and services.

## 7. Data deletion and choices

You can remove locally stored game data by clearing What Flag's app data in Android settings or uninstalling the app. Depending on Android backup settings, backed-up data may be restored later. To manage Google-held information or backups, use the relevant Google account and device controls.

What Flag has no Orion Theory user account to delete and no server-side gameplay profile. The app does not currently provide an in-game reset feature.

You can also:

- disable notifications through the app or Android settings;
- choose not to watch rewarded ads;
- use Android and Google Play controls to manage purchases and relevant account settings.

Google may retain information collected through its advertising or platform services under its own policies. Contact Google for requests concerning information held by Google.

## 8. Children's privacy and audience

What Flag is an educational geography game and may be used by children and adults. The app's intended audience and Google Play target-audience declaration must be determined consistently with its actual design and distribution.

The app does not ask players to submit personal information to Orion Theory. However, Google advertising services may process information as described above.

Parents or guardians with questions may contact Orion Theory using the details in this notice.

## 9. Security

What Flag stores game data in app-private storage. The app itself does not encrypt its local Hive database. Android's device-level security may provide additional protection. Google services use their own security measures, described in Google's documentation.

No method of storage or transmission is completely secure.

## 10. International processing and third parties

Google may process information in locations outside your country under its own policies and safeguards. Orion Theory does not operate the Google advertising or Google Play payment infrastructure.

The third-party services used in the current version include Google Mobile Ads, Google Play Billing, and Android's Auto Backup functionality.
